## 📝 Lecture Summary (At a Glance)

*   **Value Creation:** While physical products create value through tangible ownership, **services** provide economic value in an intangible manner.
*   **Types of Services:** Services are categorized into **pure services** (where the core offering is a service) and **add-on services** (which are attached to a physical product).
*   **Economic Impact:** The service sector is critical, contributing up to 80% of the **GDP** in developed economies and generating the vast majority of modern employment.
*   **Servitization:** Companies are increasingly adding service layers to their physical products to build long-term **competitive advantage** (e.g., Rolls Royce's "Power by the Hour").
*   **Evaluation Challenge:** Because services are **intangible**, the customer's **perceived risk** is much higher than when buying a product, making them difficult to evaluate before purchase.
*   **Service Attributes:** Services are evaluated primarily through **Experience attributes** (assessed during or after the service) and **Credence attributes** (difficult to assess even after the service is consumed).

## 1. The Nature of Value Creation

### Products vs. Services
*   When humans engage in exchange, they are exchanging economic value. 
*   With products, this value is **tangible** (you can touch, sense, smell, and feel the item).
    *   *Example: Buying a mobile phone from Amazon.*
*   With **services**, the value created is **intangible** (there is no physical ownership transferred).

### Categories of Services
*   **Pure Services:** The organization's sole offering is the service itself.
    *   *Examples: Education, transportation, consultancy, and hospitality/medical services.*
*   **Add-on Services:** Services that are packaged on top of a physical product offering.
    *   *Example: Buying a laptop and purchasing an additional service or warranty package to go with it.*

## 2. The Strategic Importance of Services

Studying services is vital for businesses today due to three primary macroeconomic and strategic reasons:

*   **GDP Contribution:** Services dominate modern economies. In developed nations (like the US or European countries), services account for nearly 80% of the GDP. In developing nations like India, it is around 60%.
*   **Employment Generation:** The service sector is the largest creator of jobs in the modern economy.
*   **Competitive Advantage (Servitization):** Companies use services to differentiate themselves from competitors and create recurring revenue streams. 
    *   *Example: Rolls Royce does not just sell aircraft engines (a product); they utilize "Power by the Hour," charging airlines based on the flying hours of the engine. This transforms a product sale into an ongoing service relationship.*

## 3. The Challenge of Evaluating Services

### High Perceived Risk
*   Products are easy to evaluate because they possess "search attributes"—you can inspect them before buying.
*   Because services are **intangible**, **heterogeneous** (variable), **inseparable**, and **perishable**, you cannot evaluate them prior to the transaction.
*   This lack of physical proof significantly raises the customer's **perceived risk** and creates doubts or hesitation in the buying process.

## 4. Experience vs. Credence Attributes

Because services lack search attributes, they generally fall into two different quadrants for evaluation:

### Experience Attributes
*   These are services where the customer *can* accurately judge the quality, but only **after or during** the consumption of the service.
*   *Example: Taking a flight with Qatar Airways. You cannot judge the flight beforehand, but once the flight is over, you are in a position to confidently say whether the service was good or bad.*
*   *Example: Attending a lecture or class. By the end of the session, you know if you learned something valuable.*

### Credence Attributes
*   These are highly complex services where the customer lacks the technical expertise to evaluate the quality, **even after** the service has been fully delivered and consumed. 
*   The customer must simply rely on trust (credence) in the provider's credentials.
*   *Example: Complex medical surgeries. Unless you are a doctor, you do not have the knowledge to assess if the surgeon used the absolute best technique, even if you recover.*
*   *Example: Legal services or complex financial advising.*
